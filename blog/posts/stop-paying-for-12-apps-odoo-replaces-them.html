Executive Summary

The average SMB pays for 10–15 disconnected SaaS apps that don't talk to each other. Odoo replaces most of them — CRM, accounting, inventory, e-commerce, marketing, helpdesk, project management — in one integrated system, usually for less than you're paying for three of your current subscriptions. The subscription savings are real, but the bigger win is killing double data entry and getting one version of the truth.

Count your subscriptions

Do a quick audit. Count your subscriptions: CRM, email marketing, accounting, inventory, project management, helpdesk, e-commerce platform, HR, document signing... I'll wait. Most small businesses I talk to are paying for 10 to 15 apps, and here's the dirty secret: half of them overlap, none of them integrate properly, and your team re-enters the same data in three places.

This did not happen because anyone was careless. It happened one good decision at a time. Marketing needed email automation, so they bought the best email tool. Sales needed a CRM, so they bought the best CRM. Support needed ticketing, so they bought the best ticketing tool. Each purchase was rational. The stack they created is not. You now have twelve "best in class" apps that share nothing — no data, no logins, no consistent interface — and your team is the integration layer, copying and pasting between tabs all day.

The SaaS industry loves this. Every vendor wants to be your one more subscription. Nobody's business model is "help the customer buy fewer tools." So the stack grows by accretion, year after year, until someone finally adds it up and gets angry. That someone is usually the owner, usually during budget season, usually when they discover they are spending $2,000 a month on software that mostly holds the same customer list in twelve different formats.

The hidden cost isn't the subscriptions

The subscription fees are just the visible part. The real cost is:

  • Double data entry. Customer info in the CRM, again in accounting, again in the shipping tool. Every copy is a chance for errors — and a few minutes of someone's day, multiplied by every customer, every order, every day. The labor cost of re-entry dwarfs the subscription cost, and it never appears on any invoice.
  • Integration spaghetti. Zapier zaps holding your business together with digital duct tape. When one breaks, nobody notices for a week — and then someone has to reconstruct seven days of unsynced orders by hand. Every integration is a failure point, and a twelve-app stack has dozens of them.
  • No single source of truth. Sales says one number, accounting says another, and the truth is in a spreadsheet on someone's desktop. Every cross-departmental conversation starts with twenty minutes of reconciling which system's number is right. Decisions slow down. Arguments start. Trust in the data erodes.
  • Training overhead. Every app has its own login, its own UI, its own quirks. New hires need a week just to learn where things live. Every process document has to cover five different tools. When someone leaves, their replacement inherits a maze.

Add it up honestly — subscriptions, re-entry labor, integration maintenance, reconciliation time, training — and the twelve-app stack costs far more than twelve subscriptions. It costs operational drag on everything the company does.

What Odoo actually replaces

One system, one database, one login:

  • CRM & Sales — pipeline, quotes, orders (replaces your CRM + quoting tool). The quote becomes the order becomes the invoice with no re-entry, because it is all the same record.
  • Accounting & Invoicing — real double-entry accounting (replaces QuickBooks/Xero for many). Invoices generated from sales orders, payments matched automatically, the books always current.
  • Inventory & Purchase — warehouses, barcodes, reordering rules. Stock levels that sales can actually see when quoting, because it is the same database.
  • E-commerce & Website — built-in storefront and site builder. Web orders flow straight into inventory and accounting — no sync, no middleware, no duct tape.
  • Email & Marketing Automation — campaigns, segmentation, drip flows. Marketing segments built on real purchase history, not an exported CSV from three months ago.
  • Project & Timesheets — task tracking, billing by the hour. Project time flows to invoicing automatically.
  • Helpdesk & Knowledge Base — support tickets that link to the actual customer record. When your helpdesk ticket links directly to the customer's order history, invoice status, and lifetime value — without an integration — that's when you feel the difference.
  • HR, Time Off, Recruitment — the back-office basics. Expense reports, leave requests, and timesheets in the same system as the accounting they feed.

The point is not the feature list — any vendor can list features. The point is that they share one database. A customer's record is one record, seen by sales, support, accounting, and marketing. An order is one order, from quote to cash. There is no sync because there is nothing to sync. That is the thing twelve integrated apps can never give you: not integration, but unity.

The math

Add up your current SaaS spend. For most 10–50 person companies, it's $1,000–$3,000/month across all those apps. Odoo Enterprise for the same headcount typically runs a fraction of that — and every app shares one database, so the integration cost drops to zero.

But do the full math, not just the subscription math. Take the hours your team spends each week on double entry, reconciliation, and integration babysitting. Multiply by loaded hourly cost. That number is almost always bigger than the subscription total — and it goes to zero with consolidation. I have seen companies justify the entire migration on labor savings alone, with the subscription savings as a bonus.

Be honest about the migration cost too. Consolidating twelve apps into one is a project — data migration, training, process redesign. Budget it properly: for a 20-person company, think weeks, not days. The payback period is usually under a year on labor savings alone. After that, every month is pure savings versus the stack you had.

How to consolidate without chaos

Do not rip out twelve apps in a weekend. Sequence it: start with the apps that share the most data — usually CRM/sales and accounting — because that is where the double-entry pain is worst. Get those running in Odoo, stabilize, then migrate the next cluster. Each wave should retire at least two old apps. If a wave does not let you cancel a subscription, it was the wrong wave.

Keep the apps worth keeping. Odoo doesn't demand purity — it has an API and connectors for the tools worth keeping. If your team genuinely loves one specialized tool — the industry-specific quoting engine, the CAD integration — keep it and integrate it properly. The goal is killing the redundant 80%, not ripping out the one tool your team genuinely loves. Consolidation is a strategy, not a religion.

Frequently asked questions

Is Odoo really free?

Odoo Community is free and open source — genuinely useful for CRM, sales, and basic needs. Odoo Enterprise (the full app suite with upgrades and mobile) is paid per user per month, and still dramatically cheaper than a stack of point solutions. Try it free for 15 days: quaintbusiness.com/tryodoo.

What if I love one of my current apps?

Keep it. Odoo doesn't demand purity — it has an API and connectors for the tools worth keeping. The goal is killing the redundant 80%, not ripping out the one tool your team genuinely loves. One good integration beats twelve mediocre ones.

How hard is it to migrate from multiple apps?

It's a project, not a weekend — but it's one migration instead of maintaining twelve integrations forever. Data imports are straightforward, and you do it once. I've guided companies through it; the pattern is well understood. Sequence it in waves, validate each wave before canceling old subscriptions, and it stays manageable.

Does consolidating create vendor lock-in?

Less than you think. Your data exports cleanly, and Odoo's open-source roots mean you're never truly trapped. Compare that to your current situation: locked into a dozen vendors simultaneously, each holding a piece of your data hostage. One vendor with clean exports beats twelve vendors with twelve export formats.

How do I convince my team this isn't just IT cost-cutting?

Show them the double entry. Nobody loves re-typing the same customer into three systems. Frame consolidation as giving them their time back — fewer logins, fewer tabs, no more reconciliation arguments. The people doing the re-entry are usually the biggest supporters once they see the alternative.
