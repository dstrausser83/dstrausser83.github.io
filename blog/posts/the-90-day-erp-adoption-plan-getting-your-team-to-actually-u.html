The 90-Day ERP Plan That Doesn't Make Your Team Scream

September 24, 2026 at 1:54 PM ET · by David Strausser

Quick answer

Most teams give up on ERP tools after two weeks. Here's how to build real adoption in 90 days: start small with one process, train hands-on, make it easy to fix mistakes, and track usage. No fluff, no promises.

When I worked at a small manufacturing company, our ERP rollout was a disaster. We picked SAP Business One because it was cheap and seemed simple. But after two weeks, the sales team stopped using it. They'd just email orders instead of entering them into the system. I learned the hard way that ERP adoption isn't about installing the software-it's about getting your team to actually use it.

The biggest mistake most teams make is trying to teach everyone at once. I remember a friend who tried to train 20 people on Odoo in a single workshop. Half of them were still confused by the basics after a week. Instead, we started with one process: order entry. We picked the simplest task in the system and ran it for two weeks. By the end, the team was using it regularly.

Tracking usage is another thing people overlook. Many teams measure ERP success by how many features they've implemented, not by how much work gets done. At my last company, we tracked login frequency and completed orders. If someone logged in but didn't complete an order, we called it a "dead session." That helped us see where the real problems were.

Here's what I do differently: I focus on fixing mistakes, not perfecting the system. When the sales team couldn't enter orders, we created a quick fix-just a text box with a few fields. It wasn't a full solution, but it got them moving again. Within a week, they started using the system consistently.

A common question is, "How do I know if my team is actually using the ERP?" The answer is simple: look at the data. If someone logs in and completes an order, they're using it. If they log in but don't complete anything, they're not.

How do I pick the right process to start?

Start with the most used task, like order entry or invoice processing. Avoid complex workflows. For example, if your sales team handles most orders, focus on that first.

What if my team hates the training?

Do it in real work sessions, not meetings. Show them how to fix mistakes quickly. At my company, we ran short sessions where people tried to enter an order. If they got stuck, we fixed it together in under five minutes.

How do I know if adoption is happening?

Track login frequency and completed tasks, not just features. If someone logs in and completes an order, they're using it. If they log in but don't complete anything, they're not.

What if we have a lot of different systems?

Start with one system. For example, if you use SAP Business One for sales and Odoo for inventory, pick one process to handle first. Don't try to integrate everything at once.

This plan works because it's practical. It doesn't require perfect systems or perfect people. It just needs a little focus and a willingness to fix mistakes quickly.
