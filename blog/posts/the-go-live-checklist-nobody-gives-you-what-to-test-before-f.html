The go-live checklist nobody tells you: 5 tests to run before flipping the switch on a new ERP

2026-09-23 · by David Strausser

Quick answer

You need to test data sync, user permissions, and edge cases with real data before go-live. Skip this, and your ERP will break under real-world chaos. Most companies skip 3-5 critical tests that cause weeks of downtime.

My first ERP go-live with SAP Business One was a disaster. I thought I had it covered until the night the system crashed during a 3am invoice run. That’s why I now test five things you won’t find in any ERP manual.

The first test is data sync. You have to check if your old system’s data transfers correctly to the new one. In SAP Business One, this means verifying invoice numbers and customer details match across systems. I had a client who skipped this and found out later that a single invoice was missing, causing a week-long delay. Real data matters more than mock datasets.

The second test is user permissions. Ensure that each role has the right access. A salesperson shouldn’t see financial data. I once saw a manager trying to approve an invoice for a client they didn’t even know existed. With Odoo, permission settings are more visible but still complex. Test them early to avoid chaos.

The third test is edge cases. These are unusual scenarios that happen rarely but cause big problems. For example, what if a customer sends a payment after the system’s cutoff time? Test it with real data. During my switch from SAP Business One to Odoo, a late payment triggered a refund loop that took 48 hours to fix.

The fourth test is real-time data. Check if the system updates instantly. If there’s a delay, it can lead to double billing or missed deadlines. In SAP Business One, real-time sync was reliable but Odoo requires manual triggers for some processes. A 5-second delay in Odoo caused missed shipments once.

The fifth test is user experience. Walk through a day’s work to see where people get stuck. I found that a simple report took 15 minutes to generate. Way too long. Testing with actual users catches things you wouldn’t see in a manual review.

Most companies skip these tests because they’re too time-consuming or feel “too small.” But skipping them costs weeks of downtime. I’ve seen teams lose money because they didn’t test with real data. The good news is these tests take less than a day to run.

What’s the biggest mistake teams make?

Testing with synthetic data instead of real data. A single typo in an invoice can cascade into system crashes.

How long should testing take?

24-48 hours. Enough to catch edge cases without delaying go-live.

Can I skip testing if I’m using SAP Business One?

Only if you’ve already validated all configurations. But even then, test with real data.

This checklist isn’t about being perfect. It’s about catching problems before they become disasters. And it’s free: just a few hours of careful testing.
