Executive Summary

Odoo's automated actions are one of its best features. You can route leads, update stages, send follow-ups, create tasks, and post internal notes — all without anyone lifting a finger.

But automation is leverage. It multiplies whatever it touches. Automate a clean process and you get speed. Automate a messy one and you get mess at scale.

Here are the five mistakes that turn Odoo automation from a time-saver into a time-sink:

1. Automating a process nobody has mapped. If your team cannot describe the workflow in one paragraph, the automation will encode every ambiguity and exception into silent rules. Map it on paper first. If the paper version has three "it depends" branches, fix those before you touch Odoo.

2. Triggers that are far too broad. "When a lead is updated" fires on every field change — including the ones your automation just made. Use specific field triggers ("when Stage changes to Qualified") instead of generic ones. Broad triggers are the number one cause of automation loops.

3. No error handling on scheduled actions. A scheduled job that fails silently for three weeks is worse than no job at all. Every automated action should log to the chatter or write to a dedicated log model. If you cannot see what it did, you cannot trust it.

4. Stacked automations that contradict each other. Two rules that both act on the same trigger will race. One assigns the lead to sales, the other reassigns it to support. Audit your automated actions quarterly and look for overlapping triggers. One trigger, one owner.

5. Set it and forget it. Business processes change. The automation you built in January may be wrong by June. Put a monthly 15-minute review on someone's calendar: open the automated actions list, check the run logs, kill what is stale.

The pattern behind all five: automation does not replace process thinking. It rewards it. The teams that get the most from Odoo automation are the ones who were already disciplined about their workflows before they automated a single step.

Start small. One trigger, one action, visible logging. Get that right, then expand.
