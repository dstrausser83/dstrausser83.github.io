The Cheapest ERP Quote Isn’t the Best Deal: Why the Lowest Price Often Means the Highest Cost

September 24, 2026 at 9:55 AM ET · by David Strausser

Quick answer

The cheapest ERP quote is usually the most expensive because it’s a rushed, incomplete assessment that ignores hidden costs. Vendors who give low quotes often don’t understand your business model, leading to expensive fixes later. The real cost goes beyond the software price: it's the time, the training, and the ongoing support. Choosing the lowest price is a trap that backfires.

When you’re hunting for an ERP system, you’ll see quotes that range from $500 to $10,000. But the one that’s $500? That’s probably the most expensive deal you’ll ever sign. Here’s why.

Vendors often give cheap quotes to attract you. They want you to think they’re the best deal, so they skip the hard parts: understanding your workflow, checking if the system can handle your actual needs, or even testing it with your team. If you go with the cheapest quote, you’ll end up spending way more later - on training, customizations, or even replacing the system.

SAP Business One is a great example. If you get a quote from a vendor that doesn’t know your business, they might suggest a basic version that can’t handle your inventory or sales. Then, when you realize it’s too small, you have to pay extra for upgrades or switch systems. Same with Odoo. A cheap quote might skip integration with your existing tools, leading to hours of manual work.

The real cost of an ERP isn’t just the software price. It’s the time it takes to train your team, the cost of fixing problems after you go live, and the headaches when the system doesn’t work for your business. The cheapest quote is the one that’s the most expensive because it’s the one that saves the least money long-term.

Why do vendors give cheap quotes?

Vendors often give low quotes to win your business. They want you to think they’re the best deal, so they skip the details. This is especially common with new companies or those who aren’t experienced with ERP systems.

What happens if I choose the cheapest ERP?

You’ll likely spend more later. The system might not handle your needs, leading to expensive customizations or even a complete switch. Training and support costs add up quickly.

How do I find a quote that actually fits?

Ask for a real assessment. Don’t just go with a price. Have someone check if the system works for your business. Test it with your team. Look for vendors who understand your workflow, not just the software features.
