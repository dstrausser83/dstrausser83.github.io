Tech Consulting

Practical AI and automation for SMBs. Kill the swivel-chair work, connect your systems, and adopt technology that earns its keep.

Everybody’s selling AI right now. Most of it is noise — chatbots nobody asked for, “AI-powered” features that do what a spreadsheet did in 2019, and consultants billing you to learn on your dime.

Here’s my rule, and it’s non-negotiable: if the technology doesn’t pay for itself in time saved or money made, we don’t do it. No science projects. No buying software because the demo was pretty. We find the manual, repetitive, soul-crushing work eating your team’s week — the retyping, the copy-paste between systems, the reports built by hand every Monday — and we automate it out of existence.

I come at this as an ERP guy, which matters. I don’t see AI as a toy; I see it as a layer on top of systems that already hold your business data. Automation without a system of record is just faster chaos. We get the foundation right, then we make it smart.

FAQ

Do we need an ERP before doing AI or automation?

Not always — there are plenty of quick wins in any business. But the biggest automation payoffs come when there’s a system of record underneath: clean data, defined processes, one source of truth. If you’re running on spreadsheets, we’ll talk about whether an ERP comes first. Automating chaos just gives you faster chaos.

Will AI replace my employees?

The honest answer: it replaces tasks, not people — and the companies that do this well redeploy their people onto higher-value work instead of cutting heads. I automate the work nobody wants to do. Your team’s judgment, relationships, and creativity aren’t automatable, and I don’t pretend otherwise.

How do you price this work?

The audit and roadmap are a fixed-scope engagement — you’ll know the cost before we start. Implementation is scoped per project after the audit, so you’re never buying a black box. If the ROI isn’t there, I’ll tell you not to do the project. That’s happened, and those clients call me back.

Can you work with our existing IT person or MSP?

Absolutely — and I prefer it. I’m the strategy and architecture layer; your IT handles the day-to-day. No turf wars. The goal is a plan your team can actually run, not a dependency on me forever.

What’s the first thing you’d automate in a typical SMB?

Whatever eats the most hours for the least thought — usually it’s one of three: retyping data between systems (order entry, invoicing), hand-built recurring reports, or approval chains that live in email threads. The automation audit finds your specific version in the first pass. But the pattern is almost always the same: the work nobody wants to do, that everybody does wrong once a month, that a well-built workflow handles in seconds.
