Business Development Specialist

Partnerships, alliances, and new markets — including the US/Latin America corridor I’ve worked for 15 years.

Sales closes deals. Business development opens doors that didn’t exist before — partnerships, alliances, channels, and markets. It’s the difference between hunting one deal at a time and building relationships that produce deals for years.

I’ve done this at every level: VP of Business Development at SEIDOR, running a region at Vision33, Head of Sales at Quaint Business Solutions, and — before any of that — 15 years in Mexico, where I served as a binational advisor to Baja California’s Secretary of Tourism, helping American companies bring products into Latin America. I’m a member of the Forbes Business Development Council and the Harvard Business Review Advisory Council.

That’s an unusual combination: the enterprise BD playbook and the cross-border, cross-culture reality of actually doing business between two countries. Most BD advisors have one or the other. I bring both.

Does this sound familiar?

Your partnerships are handshakes that go nowhere. A flurry of “let’s partner!” meetings, a logo swap on a slide, and then… nothing. No pipeline, no revenue, just a contact you haven’t spoken to in eight months. I structure alliances with real economics — who brings what, who gets what, how you’ll know it’s working — because partnerships without economics are just networking with extra steps.

Your channel program is all cost, no revenue. You recruited resellers, built the partner portal, ran the enablement webinars — and the channel has produced roughly zero. Channels take 6 to 12 months to produce, and most companies either quit at month four or never designed the economics right in the first place. That’s the classic signal that the next stage of growth comes through others: partners, channels, alliances, new markets. I build the systematic, no-shortcut plan for becoming known by the right people: real value first, relationships second, asks last.

FAQ

What’s the difference between business development and sales?

Sales converts existing opportunities into revenue. Business development creates new opportunities that didn’t exist — partnerships, channels, markets, strategic relationships. Sales is this quarter; BD is the next eight quarters. You need both, and they’re different muscles. I’ve built both.

How long do partnerships take to produce revenue?

Longer than anyone wants to hear — typically 6 to 12 months before a new alliance produces meaningful pipeline. The companies that win at partnerships are the ones that invest before they need the results. If you need revenue next month, that’s a sales problem, not a BD problem.

We want to expand into Latin America. Where do we start?

With an honest assessment, not a launch plan. Market opportunity, competitive landscape, regulatory and currency realities, and whether your product actually fits how business gets done there. I’ve watched well-funded companies burn seven figures learning lessons I can hand you in an afternoon. Start with the call.

Do you make introductions?

When it’s a genuine fit, yes — my network across ERP, tech, and the US/Mexico business community is part of what you’re buying. But I don’t do introduction theater. If the fit isn’t real, I’ll tell you that instead of burning someone’s goodwill.

What does a BD engagement look like in the first 90 days?

Diagnosis first: your current partnerships, channels, and relationships — what’s producing, what’s theater. Then the strategy: target partner profiles, the economics that make alliances work, and a sequenced plan with realistic timelines. By day 90 you have a working BD function taking shape — outreach underway, early conversations happening, and honest expectations about when revenue follows. No hundred-page strategy deck that sits in a drawer.
